# AI Slop Detection Platform

## Complete Project Outline

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. The Problem

AI-generated content (images, video, audio) has grown exponentially on
social media, enabling fraud, misinformation, and identity deception.
People cannot distinguish real content from AI slop with the naked eye.
Existing tools are fragmented, inaccurate, or inaccessible to regular
users. No unified, multi-modal, explainable platform exists today that a
journalist, a concerned parent, or a fraud investigator can actually
use.

Key issues: - AI image quality (Imagen 3, Midjourney v6, Firefly 3) has
reached near-zero visual artifacts - Pixel-level forensics are becoming
obsolete as generation improves - No single platform covers images +
video + audio together - Existing tools give binary yes/no without
explaining why - Screenshots and re-uploads strip all traditional
forensic signals

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 2. What We Are Building

### Core Product

A web platform where anyone uploads a photo, video, or audio clip and
receives: - A 0--100 authenticity score with confidence band - A visual
heatmap (GradCAM overlay) highlighting suspicious regions - A
plain-language checklist explaining every flag raised - Per-domain
confidence breakdown (quantum, thermodynamic, biological signals) - A
forensic trail explaining exactly how a deception was constructed
(e.g. screenshot detection)

### Key Differentiators

-   Multi-modal: images, video, audio in one place
-   Explainable: shows *why*, not just *what*
-   Physics-based signals: not just ML guessing --- grounded in physical
    laws
-   Free for individuals, B2B API for businesses and news organizations
-   Browser extension for inline detection on any webpage (the killer
    feature)

### Target Users

-   Regular people who received a suspicious WhatsApp photo
-   Journalists fact-checking before publishing
-   HR departments verifying headshots and portfolio images
-   Legal teams dealing with deepfake evidence
-   Social media platforms (API integration)

## 4. Detection Pipeline --- 6 Layers

Every uploaded file passes through these layers sequentially. Early
layers are fast and cheap; later layers are deeper and more expensive.

### Layer 1 --- Deep Reverse Search

Cross-reference Google Images, TinEye, PimEyes (face-based), Wayback
Machine snapshots, and stock photo databases. If the image is found and
predates the claim, it is authentic. If it does not exist anywhere, that
is a weak AI signal. This is the fastest and cheapest check --- runs
first.

### Layer 2 --- Metadata & Provenance Forensics

-   Inspect EXIF data: camera model, GPS coordinates, timestamp, ISO,
    shutter speed, aperture
-   Verify C2PA cryptographic signatures (hardware-signed by camera at
    capture)
-   Check for screenshot resolution fingerprints (exact match to known
    device screen sizes)
-   Detect EXIF stripping patterns
-   Analyze compression history

Complete absence of all sensor metadata simultaneously is a strong flag.
Real photos occasionally have EXIF stripped by WhatsApp or Twitter. They
almost never have every single sensor signal absent at once ---
screenshots always do.

### Layer 3 --- Physics-Based Signal Extraction (Core Innovation)

Five signals derived from physical laws. This layer is resistant to new
AI model releases because it measures physics, not learned patterns.

**Signal 1: FFT Power Spectrum** Real natural images follow a 1/f power
law --- lower spatial frequencies have more energy than higher ones,
slope approximately −2.0 to −3.2 on a log-log plot. AI images deviate
from this slope in predictable ways. GANs specifically show
characteristic spectral peaks at their training resolution.
Implementation: 2D FFT of luminance channel → radially-averaged power
spectrum → polyfit on log-log scale → compare slope.

**Signal 2: Benford's Law on DCT Coefficients** Leading digits of DCT
(Discrete Cosine Transform) block coefficients of real images follow
Benford's logarithmic distribution (1 appears \~30% of the time, 2
appears \~17%, etc.). AI images violate this because their pixel
distributions come from learned Gaussian-like distributions rather than
multiplicative physical processes. Implementation: block DCT → extract
non-DC coefficients → chi-squared test against Benford's expected
distribution.

**Signal 3: Wavelet Kurtosis** Real images have high kurtosis in wavelet
coefficient subbands --- most coefficients near zero, occasional large
values. AI images have kurtosis systematically outside the natural range
at specific wavelet scales, revealing smoothness from neural network
upsampling. Implementation: PyWavelets db4 decomposition, 4 levels →
kurtosis of each subband → compare to natural range (3--20).

**Signal 4: Quantum & Sensor Noise (PRNU)** Every real camera sensor is
physically imperfect in a unique measurable way --- Photo Response
Non-Uniformity (PRNU) --- a noise fingerprint unique to each sensor,
like a ballistic fingerprint. AI images have no PRNU because they were
never captured by a sensor. They have statistically perfect noise ---
which is itself the tell. Implementation: denoise image using wavelet
denoising → extract noise residual → analyze PRNU structure.

**Signal 5: Double JPEG & Screenshot Detection** Screenshots and
re-saves create double-compression artifacts: periodic peaks in the
frequency domain (detectable via Fourier transform), 8×8 DCT block
boundary misalignment, and exact-screen-resolution dimensions.
Resolution fingerprinting: iPhone 15 Pro → 1179×2556, Samsung S24 Ultra
→ 1440×3088. Any match to a known device screen resolution triggers an
automatic flag.

### Layer 4 --- ML Model Inference

-   Fine-tuned EfficientNet-B4 (images) --- trained on CIFAKE dataset,
    \~90% accuracy
-   TimeSformer (video) --- trained on FaceForensics++ and DFDC dataset
-   Wav2Vec2 (audio) --- fine-tuned on ASVspoof2019 dataset, \~95%
    accuracy
-   GradCAM heatmap generation --- highlights which spatial regions
    drove the verdict
-   Computational photography pipeline fingerprinting --- distinguishes
    Deep Fusion/Night Sight from Midjourney/Stable Diffusion signatures

### Layer 5 --- Biological & Semantic Consistency Checks

**For video and portraits:** - rPPG (remote photoplethysmography):
heartbeat causes periodic skin color changes (0.7--3Hz band) in real
video. AI-generated video has no cardiovascular simulation. Extracted
via green channel averaging across facial region + bandpass filter. -
Microsaccades: real eyes make involuntary movements 1--3 times/second
(0.1--0.3 degree amplitude). AI video eyes are unnaturally still or show
smooth pursuit (wrong --- smooth pursuit only occurs when tracking
moving objects). Detected via optical flow on iris landmarks using
MediaPipe. - Corneal reflection geometry: light reflected in both eyes
must be geometrically consistent with the scene's actual light sources.
Both eyes must show the same reflection.

**For all media:** - Shadow direction vs sun position and time of day -
Reflection accuracy in mirrors, water, glasses, eyes - Light source
math: multiple shadows at different angles require multiple light
sources - Depth-of-field physics: bokeh blur must follow lens aperture
laws - Perspective geometry: parallel lines must converge at the correct
vanishing point - Vocal tract formant physics: F1--F4 formants must be
consistent with a physically realizable vocal tract length (14--20cm for
adults)

### Layer 6 --- Ensemble Voting & Fusion

-   3--5 independent models vote on the verdict
-   XGBoost classifier takes the full feature vector from all layers →
    outputs a final probability
-   If models disagree significantly, verdict is flagged "Inconclusive"
    --- honesty over false certainty
-   Ensemble fusion is a lightweight model (200KB) running on CPU in
    under 5ms

## 5. Output Format (What the User Sees)

-   **Authenticity score**: 0--100 with confidence band (e.g. "73 ± 8,
    likely real"). Not a binary yes/no.
-   **Verdict label**: "Likely real" / "Inconclusive" / "Likely
    AI-generated" with confidence level
-   **Visual heatmap**: GradCAM red-to-blue overlay showing which
    regions triggered suspicion. Not just "something is wrong" but
    "these teeth are physically wrong, this shadow doesn't match the
    light source."
-   **Signal checklist**: plain-language list --- "No EXIF data found.
    Skin noise pattern matches Midjourney v6 signature. Ear geometry
    inconsistent with human anatomy."
-   **Per-domain confidence**: "Quantum signals: 87% consistent with
    real sensor. Thermodynamic: 34% real. Biological: 12% real."
-   **Forensic trail** (for screenshots): "Image dimensions match iPhone
    15 Pro screen exactly (1179×2556). Double JPEG compression detected.
    Zero EXIF sensor data present. Rendering antialiasing consistent
    with iOS Safari. Crop boundary misalignment detected."
-   **Processing time** shown to user for transparency

## 6. Mobile Photo Problem --- Special Handling

Modern phones (Pixel 9, iPhone 16) apply 40--60 AI processing algorithms
(HDR fusion, AI denoising, Deep Fusion, Night Sight) to every photo
before saving. This destroys pixel-level forensics. Our approach for
mobile shifts from "find what's wrong" to "find what's missing from its
physical history."

### What We Verify for Mobile

-   **Gyroscope + accelerometer**: micro-tremor of the human hand at
    capture should mathematically match the blur vector in the image
-   **GPS + cell tower constellation**: specific set of towers visible
    from that location + signal strengths + nearby WiFi networks ---
    nearly unique to a specific place and time
-   **Barometric pressure**: altitude should be consistent with claimed
    location's elevation
-   **Magnetometer**: phone orientation relative to magnetic north ---
    shadows and lighting should be consistent with actual sun position
-   **Ambient light sensor**: measured scene brightness should be
    consistent with EXIF ISO/shutter speed combination
-   **Burst photo siblings**: real photos have temporal neighbors
    (milliseconds apart) with physically consistent micro-differences

### Screenshot Detection Signals

-   Exact screen resolution match against known device database
-   Double JPEG compression (FFT periodic peaks)
-   OLED/LCD subpixel bleeding artifacts
-   Rendering engine fingerprint (Chrome, iOS Safari, Android Chrome
    leave different antialiasing patterns)
-   Complete absence of all sensor coherence simultaneously
-   Crop boundary DCT block misalignment
-   Aspect ratio GCD analysis to infer original canvas size

### What Still Defeats Us (Honest Limit)

A high-quality AI image printed on paper and photographed with a real
camera acquires real PRNU, real sensor data, real EXIF, and real burst
siblings. Detection then relies only on semantic and physics consistency
checks. This is the "analog hole" --- only C2PA hardware signing closes
it, because the paper printout cannot carry the original camera's
cryptographic signature.

## 7. The Camera Watermarking Angle (C2PA)

This is the long-term solution to the entire problem. The C2PA
(Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) standard embeds a
cryptographic certificate at the hardware level when a photo is taken.
Nikon Z9, Leica M11-P, Sony A9 III, and newer iPhones are already
implementing this.

The certificate chain says: "This pixel array was captured by sensor #X
at coordinates Y at timestamp Z, signed by the manufacturer's private
key." An AI model cannot forge this --- it doesn't have the camera
manufacturer's private key.

**Our platform's role:** - Build C2PA verification into the platform now
--- when adoption grows, we are already ready - As signed cameras become
the norm, *absence* of a C2PA signature becomes a red flag by itself -
Offer photographers a voluntary "sign your photo" tool using C2PA so
their real work is verifiable - Advocate for Apple and Google to make
Secure Enclave signing the default for every phone photo

## 8. Technical Architecture

### Backend Services

-   Language: Python
-   Framework: FastAPI
-   ML: PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, timm
-   Signal processing: NumPy, SciPy, PyWavelets
-   Media: ffmpeg-python, Pillow, OpenCV
-   Provenance: c2pa-python (wraps Rust c2pa-rs library)

-   Ensemble: XGBoost

    detection)

**Key insight:** No single method gives 100%. Stacking all of them makes
simultaneous spoofing computationally and logistically prohibitive for
average bad actors. Nation-state level attackers are a different
problem.

**The fundamental reframe:** We are not a pixel scanner. We are a
provenance verifier. The question stops being "does this image look
AI-generated?" and becomes "can this image prove it has a past?" That
framing is much harder to defeat regardless of how good generation gets.
